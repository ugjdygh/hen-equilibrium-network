Subject: [Cryptography] Horizon Equilibrium Network: a coupled design for circulation, ordering and conditionable privacy

Hi,

I've been working on a design for a ledger that stays permissionless
without making consensus power purchasable, and treats four things
usually handled separately as one problem: circulation, ordering,
privacy, and parameter change.

The paper is available at:

  https://eprint.iacr.org/<report-number>              (preferred once assigned)
  https://github.com/ugjdygh/hen-equilibrium-network/releases/tag/review-edition-v1

Abstract.  This note describes a ledger design that treats four properties as one
coupled problem: permissionless ordering whose cost is not purchasable,
privacy that is switchable per output rather than absolute, an issuance
curve that converges instead of decaying toward fee-only security, and
parameter adaptation that is bounded and delay-tolerant. Ordering uses
proof of work as a cost anchor together with a verification weight
derived from elapsed time and observable behaviour. Privacy is per-
output and visible, in transparent, shielded and selective modes.
Conditional transfers use a minimal script set with default-deny
semantics. Difficulty control is bounded and uses a breaker triggered on
cumulated amplitude within a time window. No cryptographic primitive is
new, no formal proofs are offered, no implementation exists, and no
asset is issued.

It is a design document. There is no implementation, no network and no
asset: nothing here is for sale, and no token or allocation exists.

Comments are welcome, particularly on the threat model, on the weight
mechanism (the weakest part, and one that may be replaced), and on the
leak analysis of the shielded mode. Section 6 lists the open problems
and what would settle each of them.
